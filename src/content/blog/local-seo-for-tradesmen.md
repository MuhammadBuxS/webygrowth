Local SEO for tradesmen comes down to three things Google weighs: how close you are to the person searching, how well your profile and website match the job they want done, and how well known and trusted you appear. You can influence the last two with a properly built Google Business Profile, a steady flow of genuine reviews, clear service pages and a handful of local links. There are no shortcuts, but there is a sensible order to do the work in.

## How local search actually works

When someone searches "emergency plumber" or "roof repair near me", Google usually shows two things: the map pack (a map with three businesses listed) and the standard organic results underneath. They are ranked using different signals, so you need to work on both.

Google's own guidance says local results are based mainly on three factors:

- **Relevance:** how well your profile matches what the person searched for.
- **Distance (proximity):** how far you are from the searcher, or from the area named in the search.
- **Prominence:** how well known you are, based on reviews, links, mentions and your wider web presence.

You cannot change where you are based, and you should never fake an address. That makes relevance and prominence the places to focus. Proximity is also why you may rank well in your own town and poorly three towns away, which is normal.

| | Map pack | Organic results |
|---|---|---|
| What appears | Business listings with reviews, phone and directions | Website pages |
| Driven mainly by | Google Business Profile, reviews, proximity | Website content, links, technical quality |
| Best for | "Near me" and urgent call-out searches | Research, comparison and "cost of" searches |
| Your lever | Profile quality and review activity | Service pages, location content, local links |

## Set up and optimise your Google Business Profile

For most trades, this is the single most valuable free asset you have. Treat it as a landing page, not a listing.

### Choose the right categories

Pick one accurate **primary category**, such as "Roofing contractor" or "Electrician", because it carries the most weight. Add secondary categories only for things you genuinely do. Check what your closest competitors use for their primary category, but choose based on your real business.

### Add your services and describe them properly

Add every service you offer, with a short plain description for each. "Flat roof replacement (EPDM and GRP)" is more useful than "Roofing". Write the business description for people first: what you do, where you work, how long you have been trading and any accreditations you actually hold.

### Set your service area honestly

If you work from home and customers do not visit, hide your address and set a service area. List the towns and areas you genuinely cover. A sprawling list of 40 places you rarely visit does not help you and can look spammy.

### Upload real photos

Use photos of your own work, vans, team and tools, taken on a phone and uploaded regularly. Stock images add nothing. Before-and-after shots of real jobs, with permission from the customer where the property is identifiable, build trust quickly.

### Use posts and Q&A

Posts are a light-touch way to show activity: a recent job, a seasonal reminder, an offer. They are not a magic ranking lever, so do not spend hours on them. Do seed the Q&A section yourself with the questions customers genuinely ask, such as call-out charges, areas covered and whether you offer free quotes, and answer them clearly.

## Build a reviews strategy that does not feel pushy

Reviews feed both ranking and conversion. People choose between three listings largely on stars, volume and what the reviews say. The aim is a steady trickle of genuine reviews, not a one-off burst.

- **Ask at the right moment.** Ask when the job is finished and the customer is pleased, ideally in person, then follow up by text.
- **Make it one tap.** Use your profile's direct review link and send it by WhatsApp or SMS. Every extra step loses reviews.
- **Keep the message short and personal.** "Thanks for having us today, [name]. If you were happy with the work, a quick Google review would really help a small local business. Here's the link."
- **Ask everyone.** Do not filter for only the customers you think will be positive. Google's guidelines are against selective review gating.
- **Never pay or reward for reviews.** Incentivised reviews breach Google's policies and can get reviews or your profile removed.
- **Reply to every review.** Thank people by name, mention the job, and handle complaints calmly and factually. Future customers read your replies.

A simple habit works best: add the review request to your job-completion routine, the same way you would hand over an invoice.

## Service pages versus location pages

Your website needs to back up what your profile says. For trades, that means two kinds of page.

**Service pages** describe one job in depth: "Boiler installation", "Loft conversions", "End of tenancy cleaning". Each should cover what is included, how the process works, rough pricing guidance where you can give it, photos of your work and a clear way to get in touch.

**Location pages** show you serve a particular area. These are where many trade sites go wrong, by cloning one page 30 times and swapping the town name. Google is good at spotting that, and customers are too.

To avoid thin duplicate location pages:

- Only create pages for areas that matter commercially, not every village within 20 miles.
- Include something specific to the area: jobs you have completed there, typical property types, local building quirks, or common problems you see.
- Add real photos, reviews or short job notes from that area.
- Link location pages to the relevant service pages, and vice versa.
- If you cannot write at least a few hundred words that are genuinely useful, cover that area in a list on your main service page instead.

## NAP consistency and citations

NAP means name, address and phone number. Your details should be identical wherever they appear: your website, Google profile, Facebook, Yell, Checkatrade-style directories, trade body listings and so on. Small differences ("Ltd" versus "Limited", a different phone number, a missing suite number) create doubt about which details are correct.

A practical approach:

1. Decide on the exact format of your business name, address and phone number.
2. Search for your business name and phone number to find existing listings.
3. Correct or claim the ones that matter and remove duplicates.
4. Add yourself to a short list of relevant, genuine directories rather than hundreds of low-quality ones.

Citations help most as a foundation. They will not outrank a competitor with far better reviews and a better website.

## On-page essentials for a trades website

Once the profile is sorted, make sure the site does its part:

- A clear page title and heading on each service page that states the service and area, written naturally.
- Your phone number and a WhatsApp or enquiry button visible on every page, especially on mobile.
- Your address or service area, opening hours and accreditations in the footer.
- Embedded Google Map and your reviews, either quoted with permission or displayed through a reputable widget.
- Real project galleries with short descriptions of what was done.
- Fast loading and a layout that works properly on a phone, since most call-out searches happen there.
- LocalBusiness structured data so search engines can read your details clearly.

## Get local links that mean something

Links from relevant local sources signal that real people know you exist. Realistic options for trades include:

- Suppliers and manufacturers whose products you install, who often list approved installers.
- Trade bodies and accreditation schemes you belong to.
- Local sponsorships such as a junior football team or community event, where the club site links back.
- Local press, when you have a story such as a community project or charity work.
- Local business groups, chambers of commerce and networking organisations.
- Partners you work alongside, such as estate agents, letting agents or architects, who can list you as a recommended contractor.

Chasing a few of these is worth far more than buying bulk links, which carry real risk.

## Track what is working

You do not need a complex dashboard. Check these monthly:

- **Google Business Profile insights:** calls, direction requests, website clicks and the searches that found you.
- **Google Search Console:** which queries and pages bring impressions and clicks.
- **Call and enquiry tracking:** ask every new customer how they found you and record it.
- **Review count and average rating** compared with your nearest competitors.
- **Map pack position** for your main searches, checked from different spots in your area, since results shift with location.

## Realistic timelines

Profile corrections, new service pages and fixed citations can start to help within weeks. Reviews, local links and authority take longer, and months is the honest expectation, longer in competitive trades and big cities. Anyone promising a guaranteed page-one ranking is guessing. What you can promise yourself is steady, compounding progress if you keep asking for reviews, adding real project content and keeping your details accurate.
