Good Shopify store design makes it obvious what you sell, shows the product honestly, and removes every unnecessary step between a visitor and a paid order. The twelve areas below are where we see stores gain or lose sales most often, and none of them depends on having a huge budget.

## 1. A homepage with a clear hierarchy

Your homepage has one job: send people to the right collection or product quickly. Lead with a headline that says what you sell and who it is for, then give visitors a clear primary button. Below that, show your main categories, a handful of best sellers, and a short block of proof such as real reviews or delivery and returns promises.

Resist stacking every announcement, banner and offer at the top. If everything is loud, nothing is.

## 2. Collection pages that help people choose

Collection pages do most of the browsing work, so treat them as working tools rather than grids of images.

- Use consistent, high-quality product photography so the grid looks tidy.
- Show the price clearly, plus a sale price or "from" price where variants differ.
- Add filters that match how customers actually shop, such as size, material, price or use case, not every attribute you can think of.
- Write a short, useful introduction at the top of the collection. It helps customers and gives search engines context.

## 3. A product page that answers every question

The product page is where the decision happens. Each of these elements earns its place:

- **Gallery:** several angles, a close-up of detail, and an in-use shot. Add a short video if it genuinely helps. Make images tap-to-zoom on mobile.
- **Variants:** show size, colour or option choices as clear buttons rather than hidden dropdowns where you can. Make out-of-stock options visible but marked.
- **Delivery information:** state dispatch times, delivery costs and the returns policy on the page, near the add to basket button. Do not make people hunt for it.
- **Reviews:** only display genuine reviews from real customers. If you have none yet, do not fake them. Clear descriptions and good photos do the work until reviews arrive.
- **Trust:** secure payment icons, a visible contact route, and plain-English policies reassure first-time buyers.

Write the description for a sceptical customer. Lead with what the product does, then specifications, materials and care.

## 4. Mobile-first checkout

Most small shops see a large share of visits on phones, so design for the thumb first. Keep the basket easy to reach, make buttons large, and avoid pop-ups that cover the page. Shopify's checkout is already well optimised, so your job is to keep the steps before it smooth: a clear basket page, accelerated payment options such as Apple Pay or Shop Pay where they suit your customers, and no surprise costs.

Show shipping costs or a free-delivery threshold early. Unexpected charges at the last step are a classic reason for abandoned baskets.

## 5. Site speed and app bloat

A slow store loses patience, and it often loses it because of apps. Each app can load its own scripts and styles on every page, and the effect adds up.

Before installing anything, ask whether the theme can already do it, or whether a small custom section would replace the app. Review your installed apps every few months, remove those you no longer use, and check that your theme has not left old code behind. Compress images, size them sensibly, and avoid autoplaying video above the fold.

## 6. Search and navigation that match how people think

Name menu items the way customers describe your products, not the way your stockroom does. Keep the main menu short, group related collections, and make the search bar easy to find on mobile.

Look at your store's search reports in Shopify. The terms people type tell you which collections to create, which products to name differently, and what is missing from your range.

## 7. Bundles and upsells that feel helpful

Bundles and "complete the set" suggestions can lift order value when they genuinely make sense. Suggest items that go with what is in the basket, not random bestsellers. Keep offers modest and clear, and do not block the path to checkout with aggressive pop-ups. If a suggestion would not help a customer in a physical shop, it does not belong online.

## 8. Email capture that earns its place

Most visitors will not buy on their first visit, so give them a reason to leave an email address. A modest welcome offer, a back-in-stock alert or early access to new products works better than a generic "subscribe to our newsletter". Use one tidy sign-up form in the footer, plus one well-timed prompt, rather than a barrage of pop-ups. Then actually send useful emails, including abandoned basket reminders.

## 9. Post-purchase that builds repeat custom

The order confirmation page and emails are part of your design. Make the confirmation clear and reassuring, include accurate delivery expectations, and make tracking easy to find. A short follow-up asking for a review, or suggesting a related product after delivery, costs little and often brings customers back. Make returns simple to start. A confident returns process tends to reduce hesitation before purchase too.

## 10. Theme customisation versus custom sections

You do not always need a bespoke theme. A quality theme, properly configured with your branding, typography and layouts, is the right call for many small businesses and keeps costs and timelines sensible.

Custom sections make sense when the theme cannot do something you need: a size guide that fits your products, a product builder, a clearer comparison table or a distinctive landing page. We would rather add a small, targeted section than rebuild an entire theme, because it keeps the store faster, easier to update and cheaper to maintain. ## 11. SEO basics for Shopify

Shopify gives you a solid technical foundation, but it still needs good content decisions.

- **Collections:** these are often your best search landing pages. Give each one a unique title, a helpful introduction and sensible product ordering.
- **Metadata:** write a distinct page title and meta description for key collections and products rather than relying on defaults.
- **Structured data:** most good themes output basic product markup (price, availability, reviews). Check that yours does, and test it with Google's Rich Results Test after any theme change.
- **URLs and duplicates:** keep URLs clean, avoid duplicate product descriptions copied from suppliers, and write original copy for your best products.

SEO builds over months and nobody can promise rankings, but these foundations give your products the best chance of being found. If you are weighing platforms, our [WooCommerce store design](/woocommerce-store-design/) page explains when WordPress-based ecommerce may suit you better.

## 12. Measuring what matters

Do not redesign by gut feel. Track a small set of numbers and review them monthly:

- Conversion rate, overall and on mobile versus desktop
- Add-to-basket rate on product pages
- Basket and checkout abandonment
- Average order value
- Page speed on your most visited pages

Look for where people drop out, then fix that stage before moving on. Small, tested changes beat sweeping redesigns. If you are changing platforms, a careful [store migration](/store-migrations/) protects the traffic and order history you have already built.
