If your website gets visitors but few enquiries, the cause is usually a handful of fixable problems rather than a lack of traffic. Below are 15 specific mistakes we see on small business sites, each with a quick way to check your own and a short fix. You can test most of them in a few minutes with nothing more than your phone.

Work through the list with your own site open. Be honest: if you have to hunt for something, so does a customer.

## Mistakes in what visitors see first

### 1. No clear call to action above the fold

**Check:** Open your homepage on a phone and do not scroll. Can you see one obvious next step, such as "Get a free quote" or "Call us"?

**Fix:** Put one primary button in the first screen, using words that describe the outcome. Repeat it at the end of every page. Competing buttons ("Learn more", "Read our blog", "Get a quote") dilute the one action you actually want.

### 2. A vague headline

**Check:** Show your homepage to someone for five seconds. Can they say what you do and where?

**Fix:** Replace slogans with a plain statement: what you do, for whom, and where. "Boiler repairs and servicing in Leeds" beats "Excellence in every detail". Save the personality for the paragraph underneath.

### 3. Content written about the business, not the customer

**Check:** Count how many sentences on your homepage start with "We". Then count how many address a problem your customer has.

**Fix:** Rewrite the opening of each key page around the customer's situation, then explain how you help. "Your boiler has stopped and it is January" is more compelling than "We have 20 years of experience".

### 4. Poor contrast and tiny text

**Check:** View the site on your phone outdoors, or in bright light. Can you read the body text and buttons without zooming?

**Fix:** Use dark text on a light background (or the reverse) with strong contrast, body text of at least 16px, and buttons large enough to tap with a thumb. Light grey text on white looks elegant in a design mock-up and unreadable in real life.

## Mistakes that make contact difficult

### 5. A phone number that cannot be tapped

**Check:** On your phone, tap your number. Does it start a call?

**Fix:** Make the number a proper phone link so it dials with one tap, and keep it visible in the header on mobile. If you rely by email, add a tap-to-message link beside it.

### 6. Forms that ask for too much

**Check:** Count the fields on your contact form. Do you need every one of them to reply to a first enquiry?

**Fix:** Ask for name, a way to reach them, and a short message. Everything else can be asked in your reply. Each extra required field gives someone another reason to give up.

### 7. A broken or unmonitored form

**Check:** Submit a test enquiry today using a different email address from the one you use to receive them. Did it arrive? Did it land in spam? Did the visitor see a confirmation?

**Fix:** Test every form after any plugin, theme or hosting change. Send enquiries to an address you check daily, show a clear thank-you message, and consider a backup channel so a silent failure does not go unnoticed for weeks.

### 8. No follow-up after the enquiry

**Check:** What happens in the five minutes after someone submits the form? If the answer is "nothing until I see it", that is the gap.

**Fix:** Send an automatic confirmation that says when they will hear from you, then reply as quickly as you can. Someone who has just enquired is probably also contacting your competitors, and the first useful reply often wins the conversation.

## Mistakes that erode trust

### 9. No trust signals

**Check:** Look for real reviews, accreditations, insurance, guarantees and a physical address or company details within two clicks of your homepage.

**Fix:** Add genuine customer reviews (with permission), relevant accreditations, and your location or registered business details. Only use what is true and can be verified. A few honest reviews beat a wall of vague praise.

### 10. Stock photos only

**Check:** Run one of your main images through a reverse image search. If it appears on dozens of unrelated sites, visitors have probably seen it too.

**Fix:** Photograph your team, premises, vehicles and finished work, even with a phone in good light. Real images tell people there is an actual business behind the page.

### 11. Hiding prices entirely

**Check:** Can a visitor tell, roughly, whether you are in their budget?

**Fix:** You do not need a full price list. A "from" price, a typical range, or a clear explanation of what affects the cost helps the right people enquire and saves you time with the wrong ones. If you truly cannot give numbers, explain how pricing works and what happens next.

## Mistakes that stop people finding you

### 12. Slow mobile loading

**Check:** Load your site on mobile data, not Wi-Fi. If you are waiting, so are your visitors. Run it through Google PageSpeed Insights for specifics.

**Fix:** Compress and resize images, remove plugins you do not use, and use decent hosting. Our guide to [website speed and Core Web Vitals](/blog/website-speed-core-web-vitals/) walks through what to look at first.

### 13. No service area or location pages

**Check:** Search Google for your service plus a town you cover. Does any page on your site speak to that location?

**Fix:** Create a genuinely useful page for each main service and area you serve, with local detail rather than the same text with the town name swapped. Our [SEO services](/seo-services/) include this kind of structure.

### 14. Sitemap and indexing problems

**Check:** Search Google for your domain with "site:" in front of it, such as site:yourdomain.co.uk. Are your key pages listed? Is your sitemap submitted in Google Search Console?

**Fix:** Submit an XML sitemap, check that no important page is blocked by a "noindex" setting (a common leftover from the build stage), and fix any pages Search Console reports as excluded or erroring.

## The mistake that hides all the others

### 15. No analytics or lead tracking

**Check:** Can you say how many enquiries last month came from the website, and from which pages or sources?

**Fix:** Install analytics and set up conversion tracking for form submissions, calls and email taps. Without it you are guessing, and you cannot tell which of the fixes above is working. Ask everyone who contacts you how they found you, too.

## How to run this check properly

A few habits make the audit more reliable:

- **Use a real phone, not a shrunken desktop window.** Mobile problems such as small tap targets, awkward menus and slow loading only show up properly on a handset.
- **Test as a stranger would.** Open the site in a private window, without being logged in, and try to get a quote without any prior knowledge of the business.
- **Ask someone outside the business to try.** A friend or relative who has never seen the site will find friction in minutes that you have stopped noticing.
- **Write down what you find.** Keep a simple list of what is broken, how serious it is, and who will fix it, so the job does not stall.

None of this needs special tools. The goal is to see your website the way a busy customer sees it: on a small screen, in a hurry, comparing you with two or three other businesses.

## A quick way to prioritise

If the list feels long, take it in this order:

- **Enquiries that cannot arrive:** broken forms, untappable numbers, missing tracking (5, 7, 15).
- **Things every visitor sees:** headline, call to action, contrast, speed (1, 2, 4, 12).
- **Confidence builders:** trust signals, real photos, pricing guidance (9, 10, 11).
- **Long-term visibility:** location pages and indexing (13, 14).

Many of these are edits you can make yourself in an afternoon. Others, such as restructuring pages or fixing slow hosting, are easier with help.
