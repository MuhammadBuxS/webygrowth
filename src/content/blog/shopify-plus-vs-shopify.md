Shopify Plus is Shopify's enterprise-level plan, and most small and mid-sized brands do not need it. You need it when you hit a specific limit that standard Shopify cannot solve, such as custom checkout rules, serious wholesale needs, or running several regional stores. If you cannot name that limit, the better move is usually to stay on a standard plan and put the difference into traffic and conversion.

## What Shopify Plus actually adds

Shopify changes plan features from time to time, so confirm the details on its current plan pages before you commit. As things stand, these are the areas where Plus differs in practice.

### Checkout customisation and Shopify Functions

Standard plans let you brand your checkout with logo, colours and fonts. Plus goes further, with deeper checkout customisation through checkout extensions, plus custom logic built with Shopify Functions. That covers things like bespoke discount rules, custom shipping or payment rules, and extra fields or messaging at checkout. If your business genuinely needs checkout to behave differently from the default, this is Plus's strongest argument.

### Launchpad

Launchpad helps you schedule launches and sales, so prices, collections, themes and inventory changes switch on and off at set times without someone staying up at midnight. It is useful for brands running drops, flash sales or tightly timed campaigns.

### B2B and wholesale features

Plus includes built-in wholesale tools such as company accounts, customer-specific catalogues and price lists, and payment terms. Shopify has been extending some B2B functionality across plans, so check what your plan includes, but the fullest set sits on Plus. This matters if wholesale orders are a real part of your revenue, rather than a few trade customers you handle by email.

### More staff accounts

Standard plans cap the number of staff accounts. Plus allows far more, with finer permission controls. This matters once you have several people across customer service, marketing, operations and development.

### Automation: Scripts, Flow and Functions

Plus has long come with Shopify Scripts for custom cart and checkout logic, and Shopify has been moving that capability to Functions. It also includes access to automation tools such as Shopify Flow with more capability than lower plans. Together these let you automate repetitive back-office work, tag customers, trigger actions and apply custom rules.

### Dedicated support and higher API limits

Plus customers get priority support and access to a dedicated contact. API limits are also higher, which matters if you sync large catalogues or orders with ERP, warehouse, or inventory systems.

### Expansion stores and multiple stores

Plus lets you run additional stores under one organisation, for example for separate regions, currencies, brands or wholesale operations. That can suit businesses that need different catalogues, languages or compliance setups rather than one store with markets switched on.

## Signs you have outgrown standard Shopify

Look for concrete problems, not a feeling that you should look bigger.

- **Order volume is straining your operation.** You are processing a high volume of orders and manual steps are slowing you down. Volume alone is not the trigger, but volume combined with workflows you cannot automate is.
- **Wholesale is a real revenue stream.** You need customer-specific pricing, trade accounts and payment terms, and workarounds are costing hours every week.
- **You are expanding internationally.** You need separate catalogues, currencies, languages or legal setups that one store cannot handle cleanly.
- **Checkout rules are unusual.** You have conditions the default checkout cannot meet, such as complex shipping rules, restrictions by customer type, or required fields.
- **Your integrations are heavy.** You are hitting API limits or need to connect ERP, warehouse or fulfilment systems at scale.
- **Your team has grown.** You need many staff accounts with different permissions.

If two or three of these describe your business today, ask Shopify for a Plus quote and compare it with the cost of your current workarounds.

## Cost framing

Shopify quotes Plus pricing itself, it varies by business, and it starts far above the standard plans. Treat it as a business decision, not a design upgrade.

Compare these figures honestly:

- **The Plus cost** over a full year, including any app and build changes it triggers.
- **What your workarounds cost** now in staff hours, apps and errors.
- **What the same money could do elsewhere**, such as paid traffic, email and SMS, better product photography or a conversion-focused redesign.

Plus pays for itself only when the first number is smaller than the savings and revenue it unlocks. We cannot promise what that will be for your store, and anyone who does without seeing your numbers is guessing.

## Questions to ask before you upgrade

Before you request a quote, write down answers to these. They make the conversation with Shopify, and with us, far more useful.

- **Which exact limitation are we hitting today?** Name the feature, the workaround and what the workaround costs each month.
- **Can an app or a plan feature solve it for less?** Many problems that look like Plus problems turn out to have a cheaper fix.
- **What will the move change for customers?** If shoppers will not notice or benefit, the case rests entirely on internal savings.
- **Who will manage the extra capability?** Custom checkout logic and automation need someone to build, test and maintain them.
- **What is the plan if growth stalls?** Be clear about the commitment you are taking on and how you would review it after a year.

If you can answer these with specifics, you are making a decision from evidence. If the answers are vague, that usually means it is too early.

## Design implications for a Plus build

The storefront theme works much like standard Shopify, but the planning is broader.

- **Checkout design becomes part of the brief.** If you are paying for checkout control, design it properly rather than leaving it at the default.
- **Wholesale needs its own journey.** Trade buyers want fast reordering, clear pricing and account views, not a retail homepage.
- **Multi-store consistency.** Regional stores need shared design systems with room for local content, currency and language.
- **Governance.** With more staff and more apps, theme structure and clean code matter, so changes do not break things.

## When the honest answer is: stay on standard Shopify

For many brands, the honest answer is not to upgrade. Stay on standard Shopify if:

- You cannot name a specific limitation Plus would fix.
- Your problem is traffic or conversion, not platform capability.
- Wholesale is occasional and manageable with a few apps.
- Your checkout works well enough and customers are completing orders.

In that case, take the Plus budget and invest it in what moves revenue: clearer product pages, faster mobile performance, better photography, email flows and search visibility.
