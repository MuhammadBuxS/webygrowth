The best website platform for a UK small business depends on what the site must do. WordPress suits most service businesses that want to generate enquiries and rank in search, Shopify or WooCommerce suit businesses that sell products online, and Wix or Squarespace suit very simple sites you will run yourself. Decide the job first, and the platform choice usually becomes obvious.

## Which website platform should a small business choose?

Choose the platform that matches the main job of your website: WordPress for enquiries and content, Shopify for a focused online store, WooCommerce for a store inside a content-led site, and a website builder for a simple site managed in-house. There is no universally best platform, only a best fit for a particular business.

Here is the short version in one table:

| Platform | Best for | Ownership and portability | SEO control | Ease of editing | Scaling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WordPress | Service businesses, content-led sites, local SEO | High: open source, you own the files | Very high | Good once set up | Very flexible |
| WooCommerce | Stores that also need strong content and SEO | High: open source, you own the files | Very high | Moderate, more to manage | Flexible, needs good hosting |
| Shopify | Stores where selling is the main job | Moderate: hosted, products exportable | High for stores | Very easy | Strong, handles growth well |
| Wix / Squarespace | Simple brochure sites, small low-volume shops | Lower: closed systems | Basic to moderate | Very easy | Limited |

## Who is WordPress best for?

WordPress is best for service businesses, local firms and anyone who wants full control over content and search performance. It is open source, so you own your site and any competent developer can work on it. It has the most flexible SEO capability of the options here, which suits trades, clinics, consultants and professional services.

The trade-off is responsibility. A WordPress site needs hosting, updates and security care, either handled by you or by a support arrangement. Done properly, it is a dependable foundation. Our [WordPress website design](/wordpress-website-design/) service covers the build, the SEO structure and the handover.

**Choose WordPress if:**

- You want enquiries, calls or bookings rather than direct sales
- Organic search and local SEO matter to your growth
- You want to own your site and keep your options open
- You plan to publish regular content, such as guides or case studies

## Who is Shopify best for?

Shopify is best for businesses whose main job is selling products online and who want a hosted system that handles payments, checkout and growth with little technical upkeep. It is built for ecommerce, so inventory, shipping, discounts and abandoned-cart tools are part of the platform rather than add-ons.

The trade-offs are that it is a hosted, closed system, you pay ongoing subscription and app costs, and content-led SEO is less flexible than on WordPress. For most stores that is a fair price for reliability. See our [Shopify store design](/shopify-store-design/) service for how we approach builds.

**Choose Shopify if:**

- Selling products is the main purpose of the site
- You want checkout, payments and inventory handled in one hosted system
- You prefer less technical maintenance
- You expect to grow your catalogue or order volume

## Who is WooCommerce best for?

WooCommerce is best for businesses that want an online shop that sits inside a WordPress site, with strong content and full ownership. It is the ecommerce plugin for WordPress, so you get WordPress's SEO flexibility and can shape the shop to unusual requirements.

It asks more of you than Shopify. You manage hosting, security, updates and extensions, and larger catalogues need good hosting to stay fast. Our [WooCommerce store design](/woocommerce-store-design/) service is aimed at businesses who want that control.

**Choose WooCommerce if:**

- You want a shop and a content-rich site in one place
- You need custom features or unusual product rules
- Owning the code and data is important to you
- You are comfortable with, or will pay for, technical care

## Who are Wix and Squarespace best for?

Wix and Squarespace are best for simple sites that you will build and manage yourself, such as a small brochure site, a portfolio or a very small shop. They bundle hosting, templates and editing in one subscription, which makes them quick to launch and easy to update.

They are not the wrong answer for everyone. A new business with a tight budget and a handful of pages may be well served by one. The limits are less SEO control, less flexibility as you grow, and weaker portability, since you generally cannot lift your site and move it to another host as it stands.

**Choose Wix or Squarespace if:**

- You want a simple site live quickly and will manage it yourself
- Your needs are modest and unlikely to grow much
- You prefer one subscription over managing hosting
- Search traffic is not central to how you win customers

## How do ownership and portability compare?

Ownership is highest on open-source platforms. With WordPress and WooCommerce you own the files, the database and the content, and you can move hosts or developers freely. Shopify lets you export products and customer data, but the theme and platform are tied to Shopify. Wix and Squarespace let you export some content, but the design and structure generally cannot move intact.

This matters because a platform you cannot leave is a platform that sets the terms. It is worth asking, before you commit, what it would take to move in three years.

## How do the platforms compare for SEO?

WordPress and WooCommerce give the most control over SEO, covering page structure, URLs, metadata, schema, redirects and site speed. Shopify performs well for stores and handles the technical basics reliably, though it has fixed URL patterns. Wix and Squarespace cover the essentials but offer less fine control.

No platform ranks you on its own. Rankings come from page structure, content quality, speed and local signals, which is why SEO should be planned into the build. Our [SEO services](/seo-services/) work on that structure whichever platform you use.

## What are the running costs to expect?

Every platform has running costs, though they differ in kind rather than in a single fixed figure. In general terms:

- **WordPress and WooCommerce:** hosting, a domain, maintenance and updates, and any paid plugins or themes. Costs depend on your hosting level and how much care you buy.
- **Shopify:** a monthly platform subscription, transaction and payment fees, a domain and often paid apps.
- **Wix and Squarespace:** a monthly or annual plan that bundles hosting, plus a domain and any extras.

Rather than compare headline prices, add up the whole cost over a year or two, including developer time, apps and support. Our guide to [small business website cost in the UK](/blog/small-business-website-cost-uk/) explains how to do this.

## What happens if you need to migrate later?

Moving between platforms is possible, but it needs planning. Products, pages and customer data can generally be transferred, whereas the design, some features and your URL structure often have to be rebuilt or mapped. The main risk is lost search visibility if redirects are missed.

Migration is easier from open platforms than from closed ones, and easier when your content is well organised. If you are considering a move, our [store migrations](/store-migrations/) service covers planning, redirects and testing.

## What mistakes do businesses make when choosing a platform?

The most common mistake is choosing a platform before deciding what the site must do. Others follow from it:

- **Picking on price alone.** A cheap start can become expensive if you outgrow the platform.
- **Choosing what a friend uses.** Their needs are not yours.
- **Ignoring ownership.** You find out the cost of leaving only when you want to leave.
- **Skipping SEO planning.** Retrofitting structure later is harder than building it in.
- **Overbuilding.** A simple site on a simple platform beats a complex build you cannot maintain.
